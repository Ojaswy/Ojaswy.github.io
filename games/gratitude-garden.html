About this garden

Tend your garden by naming things you're grateful for — typed or spoken. Each gratitude waters a plant and helps it grow from seed → sprout → bud → bloom. Your garden becomes a living record of your good things.

Your plants never die. If a few quiet days pass, the garden simply rests and wilts a little. The moment you return with one gratitude, it springs back to life. No streaks to break, no guilt — just an invitation to notice the good.

The science (honest version)

Regularly noting what you're grateful for is one of the better-supported ways to nudge everyday positive mood (Emmons & McCullough, 2003; Seligman's "Three Good Things," 2005). Two practical findings shaped this app: variety matters (the same gratitude every day loses its spark), and doing it flexibly beats forcing a daily quota — so prompts rotate and the garden never punishes a missed day. Benefits are real but modest; this is a gentle wellbeing habit, not a treatment for depression or anxiety.
